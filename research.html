Research

My interdisciplinary research field – at the intersection of machine learning, electronics and signal processing – offers a vast choice of interesting research directions. Here are some of the most exciting projects I have worked on.

Computer vision

High-speed human action recognition in video

The first implementation of video action classification on a physical reservoir computer. This project was particularly exciting because I was in charge of the full pipeline: human detection, pose estimation, feature extraction and classification, neural network training, development of new post-processing strategies, benchmarking with other state-of-the-art systems, hardware design for high speed processing.

Picco, E., Antonik, P. & Massar, S. "High speed human action recognition using a photonic reservoir computer." Neural Networks 165 (2023): 662–675. [DOI] [arXiv]

Audio

Real-time speech recognition on a physical neural network

In this project we used deep neural architectures to classify spoken digits. The classification run on an optoelectronic system in real time, with a deep architecture whose layers were connected analogically rather than digitally, for the first time on this class of hardware.

Picco, E., Lupo, A. & Massar, S. "Deep photonic reservoir computer for speech recognition." IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems 36.4 (2024): 7606–7614. [DOI] [arXiv]

Method

Optimising physical neural networks with a delayed input

Physical learning systems are awkward to tune because their internal parameters are not freely accessible. We showed a much easier way to optimise performance: with only a simple manipulation of the input signal in time.

Picco, E., Jaurigue, L., Lüdge, K. & Massar, S. "Efficient optimisation of physical reservoir computers using only a delayed input." Nature Communications Engineering 4.1 (2025): 3. [DOI] [arXiv]

Hardware

Data acquisition and control systems

I am comfortable with sensors, embedded systems and messy real-world signal acquisition, not only with clean datasets. All of the projects above needed an acquisition layer that did not exist. I designed from scratch the FPGA and hardware to interface analog optical signals with digital processing plus the instrumentation and control systems around them. I extended the same work as a visiting researcher at IBM Research Zurich, driving a neuromorphic accelerator.
